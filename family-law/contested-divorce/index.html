Contested Divorce in Lucknow: Grounds, Stages and Responding to a Notice

“My spouse will not agree to a divorce. Can I still get one?” Yes, if you can prove a ground the law recognises. A contested divorce is a full case in which the court decides on evidence. This page explains the grounds, each stage, and what to do if you are the one who has received the notice.

Contested Divorce in Lucknow: Grounds, Stages and Responding to a Notice

The grounds a court can act on

A contested divorce must rest on a ground set out in the law that governs the marriage. Under section 13 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, the grounds available to either spouse include that the other has:

  • treated the petitioner with cruelty — conduct of such a kind that the petitioner cannot reasonably be expected to continue living with the other spouse
  • deserted the petitioner for a continuous period of at least two years before the petition, without reasonable cause and without consent
  • had voluntary sexual intercourse with someone else after the marriage
  • ceased to be a Hindu by converting to another religion
  • been incurably of unsound mind, or suffered from a mental disorder of such a kind that the petitioner cannot reasonably be expected to live with them
  • renounced the world by entering a religious order, or not been heard of as alive for seven years or more

A divorce can also be sought where, after a decree of judicial separation or of restitution of conjugal rights, the spouses have not resumed living together for one year. A wife has some additional grounds under the Act. The Special Marriage Act, 1954 (section 27) has a similar list. Muslim wives may seek dissolution under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939, and Christians under the Divorce Act, 1869, each on the grounds those Acts set out.

How a contested divorce proceeds

  • Petition. The petitioner files a petition in the Family Court with jurisdiction, setting out the marriage, the facts relied on and the ground, supported by an affidavit.
  • Notice. The court issues notice (summons) to the other spouse, the respondent, with a copy of the petition and a date to appear.
  • Counselling and mediation. The Family Court is required to try to bring the parties to a settlement, and usually refers them to counselling or to mediation early on. If they settle, the case can be converted into a mutual consent petition.
  • Written statement. If the case continues, the respondent files a written reply, admitting or denying each statement and setting out their own version. Claims such as maintenance during the case are often made by separate application at this stage.
  • Issues and evidence. The court frames the questions it must decide. Each side gives evidence — usually by affidavit, followed by cross-examination by the other side — and produces documents and witnesses.
  • Arguments and judgment. After evidence, both sides argue and the court decides whether the ground is proved. If it is, a decree of divorce follows. An appeal lies to the High Court within the time the law allows.

A contested divorce usually takes considerably longer than a mutual consent divorce, and its length depends on the court, the number of witnesses and the conduct of both sides. Case status and dates for the Lucknow courts can be checked on the Lucknow district court’s eCourts website.

If you have just received a divorce notice

  • Do not ignore it. If you do not appear on the date given, the court can proceed in your absence (ex parte) and may grant the divorce without hearing you.
  • Read the petition carefully and note the date of appearance, the court, the case number and the ground alleged.
  • Write down your own account of each allegation while events are fresh, with dates, and collect anything that supports it: messages, emails, photographs, medical records, and the names of people who know the facts.
  • Consider what you want. You may oppose the divorce, agree to a divorce on terms, or seek maintenance, custody or the return of belongings. The written statement is where your position is first put on record.
  • Keep to the time limits the court gives for filing the reply and documents. Delay can lead to your right to file being closed.

Document checklist

  • Proof of marriage, and the children’s birth certificates
  • The petition, notice and every order received, if you are the respondent
  • A dated account of the events relied on
  • Supporting evidence: messages, emails, letters, photographs, medical and police records
  • Names and contact details of witnesses
  • Income and expense records if maintenance or alimony is in issue
