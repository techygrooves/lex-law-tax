Court Marriage & Marriage Registration in Lucknow

Two different things people call “court marriage”

In everyday speech, “court marriage” usually means a marriage solemnised before a government Marriage Officer under the Special Marriage Act, 1954. That Act is a civil marriage law: it applies whatever the religion of the couple, and does not need any religious ceremony. It is the usual route for couples of different faiths, and for any couple who prefer a civil marriage.

Registration is something else. A marriage solemnised by religious ceremony — for example by Hindu rites, including the saptapadi (the seven steps around the sacred fire) — is valid once the ceremony is performed. Registration records that marriage officially and gives the couple a certificate. In Uttar Pradesh, registration of marriages has been compulsory since the State notified the Uttar Pradesh Marriage Registration Rules, 2017 on 3 August 2017.

“We want to get married. Where do we begin?”

Under the Special Marriage Act, both parties must be unmarried (no living spouse), capable of giving valid consent, outside the degrees of relationship the Act prohibits, and of age: 21 for the man and 18 for the woman. The steps are set by the Act itself.

  • Notice of intended marriage. The couple give written notice, in the form set out in the Act, to the Marriage Officer of the district in which at least one of them has lived for at least 30 days immediately before the notice. For a marriage in Lucknow, that means at least one of you must have lived in Lucknow district for the 30 days before giving notice.
  • Publication and objections. The Act provides for the notice to be entered in the Marriage Notice Book and published, and for anyone to object within 30 days on the ground that a legal condition is not met. In Uttar Pradesh, the Allahabad High Court held in January 2021 (Safiya Sultana v. State of U.P.) that publication of the notice and the call for objections are not compulsory: the Marriage Officer publishes the notice only if the couple ask for it in writing. If you do not ask, the officer does not publish it or entertain objections, and proceeds to the solemnisation after checking identity, age and consent.
  • Declarations and solemnisation. On the date fixed, both parties and three witnesses sign declarations in the form the Act prescribes, in the presence of the Marriage Officer. The marriage is solemnised at the Marriage Officer’s office, or at another place nearby, in any form the parties choose, provided each says to the other, before the officer and the witnesses, that they take the other as their lawful spouse.
  • Certificate. The Marriage Officer enters the marriage in the Marriage Certificate Book, signed by the couple and the three witnesses. That entry is conclusive evidence that the marriage was solemnised under the Act.

Ask the Marriage Officer’s office in advance how it schedules the solemnisation date and which identity and address documents it accepts, because offices apply their own working arrangements to the Act’s requirements. The full text of the Act is on India Code.

“We are already married. How do we register our marriage?”

Marriages in Uttar Pradesh are registered online through the marriage registration page of the Uttar Pradesh Stamp and Registration Department. The steps below are taken from the department’s own instructions on that page.

  • Fill in the application form in both Hindi and English — the portal requires both.
  • Upload a recent photograph of each spouse (the portal asks for a JPG under 40 KB) and each spouse’s identity proof, age proof and residence proof (PDF, up to 70 KB each). Give the same address as the one on the residence proof you upload.
  • Upload the identity, age and residence proof of two witnesses.
  • Upload the affidavit of the bride and groom, in the format the portal provides, after it has been attested by a notary. The portal also provides a format for an affidavit from the religious authority who performed the marriage.
  • Check the preview, save the application, and keep the application number and password it gives you.
  • Pay the fee online and keep the payment receipt.
  • Within 30 days of completing the application, go to the registration office you selected, with all the original documents.

The portal warns that if an attached certificate or affidavit is found to be incorrect, the registration certificate can be cancelled — so the documents must match each other and the facts. The portal does not publish the fee on its instructions page; it is shown during the application. If you would rather register an existing marriage under the Special Marriage Act, section 15 of that Act allows it where the conditions it lists are met, after notice to the Marriage Officer and the period it provides for objections.

Document checklist

  • Identity proof for each of you (the online route relies on Aadhaar-based verification)
  • Age proof for each of you, such as a school-leaving or birth certificate
  • Residence proof for each of you; for a Special Marriage Act notice, proof that one of you has lived in Lucknow district for the last 30 days
  • Recent photographs
  • Two witnesses for registration of an existing marriage, or three for a marriage under the Special Marriage Act, each with identity, age and address proof
  • For registration of an existing marriage: the notarised affidavit, and evidence of the ceremony such as the invitation card and photographs
  • If either of you was married before: the divorce decree, or the death certificate of the former spouse

Common problems to avoid

  • Mismatched details. A name, date of birth or address spelt differently across documents is the most common reason an application is held up. Correct it before applying, not at the counter.
  • The wrong district. A Special Marriage Act notice must go to the Marriage Officer of a district where one of you has actually lived for the last 30 days.
  • An earlier marriage not formally ended. A person whose earlier marriage has not been dissolved by a decree cannot marry again under any of these laws; the second marriage would be void and may be an offence.
  • Leaving it late. Registration is compulsory in Uttar Pradesh. A certificate is usually needed for a passport, a visa, a bank account in a joint name, insurance and many other purposes, and is far easier to obtain soon after the wedding.
