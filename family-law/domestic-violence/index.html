Domestic Violence & Protection Orders: Applying and Responding

“I am not safe at home. What can the law do now?” The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 lets a woman ask a Magistrate for orders that protect her, secure her right to live in the home, and provide money for her needs, quickly if necessary. If you are in immediate danger, call 112 first.

Domestic Violence & Protection Orders: Applying and Responding

If you are in danger now

  • Call 112, the national emergency number, and go to a safe place.
  • If you are injured, get medical treatment and ask for the record to be kept; it may be important later.
  • Free legal services are available to women regardless of income under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. The National Legal Services Authority runs a helpline on 15100, and the District Legal Services Authority for Lucknow can arrange a lawyer.

What the law treats as domestic violence

The Act protects a woman who is or has been in a domestic relationship with the respondent — a wife, a woman in a relationship in the nature of marriage, a mother, sister, widow or daughter living in the same household. Domestic violence under the Act is not limited to physical violence. It includes:

  • physical abuse — hurting or endangering health or safety
  • sexual abuse
  • verbal and emotional abuse — insults, ridicule, name-calling, threats, including about not having a son
  • economic abuse — depriving her of money or resources she is entitled to, disposing of her belongings or stridhan, or restricting her access to the household
  • harassment to meet an unlawful demand for dowry or property

The orders a Magistrate can make

Relief under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005
OrderWhat it doesSection
Protection orderProhibits the respondent from committing violence, contacting her, entering her workplace or the children’s school, or operating joint accounts18
Residence orderProtects her right to live in the shared household; can stop her being dispossessed, or direct alternative accommodation19
Monetary reliefCovers expenses and losses: medical costs, loss of earnings, damaged property, maintenance for her and the children20
Custody orderTemporary custody of the children, with any arrangements for the respondent’s contact21
CompensationCompensation for injuries, including mental torture and emotional distress22
Interim and ex parte ordersOrders made urgently, before the case is decided, and if necessary without first hearing the respondent23

Breach of a protection order, or an interim protection order, is an offence under section 31 of the Act. The full text of the Act is on India Code.

If you are considering an application

  • The application is made to the Magistrate where you live (even temporarily), where the respondent lives, or where the violence took place.
  • A Protection Officer, appointed by the State Government for the district, can help you record a Domestic Incident Report and file the application, and can arrange shelter and medical help. The court, the police or the legal services authority can put you in touch.
  • Write down incidents with dates, and keep messages, photographs and medical records somewhere the respondent cannot reach them.
  • Applying under the Act does not prevent a criminal complaint, a maintenance claim or a divorce case; the remedies can run together.

If you have received notice of an application

  • Read the application and any interim order carefully. If a protection or residence order has been made, comply with it even if you dispute the allegations: breach is an offence.
  • Appear on the date given. The Magistrate can proceed in your absence.
  • File a reply that answers each allegation specifically, with your own evidence. Bare denials carry little weight.
  • Keep any contact with the applicant within what the order allows, and in writing where possible.

Document checklist

  • A dated list of incidents
  • Photographs of injuries and damage; medical records and prescriptions
  • Messages, emails, call records and recordings
  • Any police complaint or Domestic Incident Report
  • Proof of marriage or of the relationship, and of the shared household
  • Income and expense records for monetary relief
  • Names of witnesses
