Maintenance & Alimony: Interim, Permanent and Child Maintenance

“How will I and the children manage while this is going on, and afterwards?” The law provides for maintenance during a case, for alimony when it ends, and for children throughout. This page explains the different claims, the records a court will ask for, and what to do if circumstances change or payments are missed.

Maintenance & Alimony: Interim, Permanent and Child Maintenance

The different kinds of maintenance

Maintenance is money paid regularly by one person for another’s living expenses. Alimony usually means the amount fixed for a spouse when a matrimonial case ends, either as a lump sum or as periodic payments. Several laws provide for these claims, and more than one may be used, although a court takes into account what has already been awarded in another proceeding.

Where maintenance can be claimed
LawWho can claimWhen
Section 144, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (formerly section 125, Code of Criminal Procedure)A wife unable to maintain herself, children, and parentsAt any time, before the Magistrate or Family Court, whether or not another case is pending
Section 24, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955Either spouse without sufficient independent incomeWhile a matrimonial case is pending (maintenance and the costs of the case)
Section 25, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955Either spouseWhen a decree is passed, or afterwards (permanent alimony)
Sections 18 and 20, Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956A Hindu wife; children and aged parentsAs an independent claim
Section 20, Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005A woman facing domestic violence, and her childrenAs monetary relief in a domestic violence application
Sections 36 and 37, Special Marriage Act, 1954The wife (during the case and on the decree)In proceedings under that Act

The Supreme Court held in July 2024 (Mohd. Abdul Samad v. State of Telangana) that a Muslim woman, including a divorced Muslim woman, may also claim maintenance under the general provision now in section 144, in addition to her remedy under the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986.

Interim maintenance, permanent alimony and child maintenance

  • Interim maintenance is paid while the case is being decided, so that the claimant is not left without support for its duration. It is decided on the affidavits and documents, without a full trial. Under section 144, an application for interim maintenance is to be decided, as far as possible, within sixty days of notice being served.
  • Permanent alimony is fixed when a divorce or other decree is passed, taking into account each spouse’s income and property, their conduct, the standard of living during the marriage, and other relevant circumstances. It can be a single settlement amount or a monthly payment.
  • Child maintenance belongs to the child. It covers food, housing, clothing, schooling and medical needs, and is ordinarily paid to the parent with whom the child lives. Agreement between the parents does not take away the court’s power to ensure that the child is provided for.

Income and expense records the court will expect

The Supreme Court in Rajnesh v. Neha (2020) directed that in maintenance cases both parties file an affidavit disclosing their assets and liabilities, in a standard format, and that the respondent file the reply and affidavit within the time the court allows. A party who conceals income can face adverse consequences. Keep the following ready:

  • Salary slips, Form 16 and income-tax returns for the last few years
  • Bank statements for all accounts
  • Details of property, investments, vehicles, loans and EMIs
  • Business records if self-employed: accounts, GST returns, ledgers
  • Monthly expenses: rent, household costs, school fees, medical costs, with bills where possible
  • Proof of the standard of living during the marriage

If you have received a maintenance application

  • Note the date of appearance and appear on it; orders can be passed in your absence.
  • Prepare the affidavit of assets and liabilities fully and truthfully. Understating income is usually discovered through bank statements and tax records, and damages credibility on everything else.
  • Set out your own expenses and responsibilities, such as dependent parents or loans, with documents.
  • If you say the applicant has sufficient income of their own, produce evidence of it.

If circumstances change or a payment is missed

Change of circumstances. An order for maintenance is not fixed forever. Either party can ask the court to increase, reduce or cancel it if there is a change in circumstances — a job lost, a significant increase in income, remarriage, or a child coming of age. Under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita this is done under section 146 (formerly section 127 of the Code of Criminal Procedure).

Missed payments. If the person ordered to pay fails to pay without sufficient cause, the claimant can apply to the court to recover the arrears. The court can issue a warrant to recover the amount as if it were a fine and, for each month of default, can order imprisonment for up to one month or until payment is made. The application must be made within one year from the date the amount became due, so arrears should not be allowed to build up. Keep a record of every payment received and every one missed.

Document checklist

  • Proof of marriage and the children’s birth certificates
  • Your income documents and those you have of the other party
  • A monthly budget with bills and receipts
  • School fee receipts and medical bills for the children
  • Copies of any earlier maintenance orders and a record of payments made and missed
  • Details of any other case between the parties

Questions

Questions People Ask

Earning an income does not by itself rule out maintenance. The question is whether the income is sufficient to maintain her in the standard of living the marriage provided. The court looks at both parties’ incomes and responsibilities.

Under sections 24 and 25 of the Hindu Marriage Act, either spouse may claim. Section 144 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita provides for a wife, children and parents, not a husband.

The Supreme Court in Rajnesh v. Neha directed that maintenance be awarded from the date of the application. The amount is then calculated from that date even if the order is passed later.

A one-time permanent alimony amount agreed in a divorce and recorded by the court ordinarily settles the spouse’s claim. Child maintenance is treated differently because the right belongs to the child.
