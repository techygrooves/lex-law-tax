Gift Deed Stamp Duty in Uttar Pradesh: Who Qualifies for the ₹5,000 Concession?

A practical guide to eligible relatives, property categories, the five-year condition and costs beyond stamp duty, based on the official January 2026 notification.

By H.R. Legal Associate · Published 17 October 2026 · 12 minute read

  2. Eligible relatives
  3. Covered property
  4. The five-year condition
  5. Stamp duty and total cost
  6. Document checklist
  7. Registration steps
  8. Common questions
  9. Official sources

A parent wants to transfer a house to a daughter. A husband wants to gift his share of a flat to his wife. A shop owner wants to pass a commercial property to a son without waiting for succession. In each situation, the first question is often the same: “Can we register the gift for ₹5,000?”

Uttar Pradesh does provide a substantial stamp-duty concession for certain family gift deeds. However, eligibility depends on the relationship, the parties, the property and its recent gift history. The ₹5,000 figure is a maximum stamp-duty amount under the notification; it is not an assurance that every expense connected with the transfer will fit within that sum.

This guide explains the notification dated 8 January 2026, the relatives it identifies, and the practical checks to complete before preparing a gift deed in Lucknow or elsewhere in Uttar Pradesh. The official notification, rather than a generic list of “blood relatives”, should be the starting point.

The short answer: who qualifies?

The notification covers gifts to the donor's son, daughter, father, mother, husband, wife, daughter-in-law, real brother, real sister, son-in-law, grandchildren through a son or daughter, and the widow of a deceased real brother. Eligible residential, agricultural, commercial and industrial properties are covered. Both parties must be natural persons, and the other conditions must be satisfied. [1]

Read that list from the donor's perspective: who is receiving the property from this particular owner? “We are close relatives” is not a complete eligibility explanation. Identify the exact relationship and collect the documents supporting it before purchasing stamps or finalising the deed.

Which notification should you check?

The relevant document is Uttar Pradesh Government Notification No. 1/2026/26/94-2-2026-700(29)/2021, dated 8 January 2026, issued under section 9 of the Indian Stamp Act as applicable in the state. It supersedes the specified August 2023 notification, subject to its saving clause, and operates from publication in the Official Gazette until further orders. It remits duty above the stated ₹5,000 maximum on qualifying instruments. [1]

The official Stamp and Registration Department website lists the family gift-deed concession and links to the order. Keep a copy of the document used for your transaction and recheck the departmental position near execution. A saved newspaper headline from an earlier year should not be the only basis for deciding the amount payable. [2]

Eligible relationships: how to read the list correctly

Recipient's relationship to the donorPractical point to check
Son or daughterMatch identity details and the documents establishing parentage.
Father or motherCheck the relationship in this direction of transfer.
Husband or wifeKeep appropriate evidence of the marriage where required.
Daughter-in-lawThe notification identifies the son's wife.
Real brother or real sisterUse the notification's precise description rather than assuming every extended family relationship is covered.
Widow of a deceased real brotherThe brother's death is material to this listed category.
Son-in-lawThe notification identifies the daughter's husband.
Grandson or granddaughterChildren of a son or daughter are expressly described.

A useful way to prepare is to draw a small family chart for the proposed donor and donee. Put the supporting record beside each link: a birth record, marriage record, family record or another document appropriate to the facts. Where spellings differ across documents, address the discrepancy before the appointment rather than hoping it will be overlooked.

Do not enlarge a concession merely because another law uses a wider definition of “relative”. Income-tax provisions, succession rules and stamp notifications serve different purposes. An exemption or relationship recognised for one purpose does not automatically settle the stamp treatment of a gift deed.

Transfers involving grandparents as recipients, cousins, nephews, nieces or other extended relations should be examined against the actual wording and any applicable clarification. Similarly, do not assume that a listed relationship automatically works in every reverse direction. If the category is not clearly available, obtain a transaction-specific determination instead of budgeting on an assumed concession.

Does the concession cover shops and industrial property?

The 2026 order expressly includes commercial and industrial property alongside residential and agricultural property. It also excludes juristic entities such as firms, companies, trusts and institutions, whether involved as donor or donee, and property owned by such entities. [1]

For a shop, check the title before assuming that a family member owns the premises personally. The person running a business from a property may not be the registered owner. The papers may reveal a company, a co-owner, a landlord, an authority allotment or another arrangement. The proposed deed must follow the actual interest held, not the name displayed on the shopfront.

For industrial and authority-allotted property, examine the allotment or lease terms and whether permission, transfer charges or other compliance is required. A stamp concession does not itself answer those questions. In Lucknow, the relevant development-authority or other allotting-body records may need separate examination, depending on the property.

Agricultural property also requires attention to tenure and applicable restrictions. Confirm the donor's transferable interest and the nature of the land. The fact that agricultural property appears among the covered categories does not remove every other legal condition affecting its transfer.

The five-year condition: a second gift needs a fresh check

If a recipient gifts onward a property received through a gift deed that used this exemption within five years of registration, that onward gift is outside this notification's concession. [1]

Consider a hypothetical family planning two transfers. A father first gifts a house to his son, and the son then wants to gift it to his wife soon afterwards. Before calculating the second deed's charges, examine the first deed, its registration date and the concession actually used. Eligibility should be assessed for each instrument, not assumed because everyone belongs to one family.

This condition should not be described as a general ban on selling gifted property for five years. The wording addresses the availability of the concession for a subsequent gift. Whether a proposed sale or another transaction is otherwise permissible is a separate question involving the title, deed terms and applicable law.

For the same reason, avoid designing a series of family transfers around a headline about low stamp duty. Start with the intended final ownership and ask whether there is a lawful, direct way to achieve it. Additional deeds can create extra cost, documentation and questions about the earlier transaction.

Is ₹5,000 the total cost of a gift deed?

No. The notification examined here concerns stamp duty. It does not, by itself, establish an all-inclusive registration price. Obtain an itemised calculation before execution, showing the applicable registration fee and other legitimate charges separately.

Cost itemWhat to ask before payment
Stamp dutyDoes this particular deed meet every condition for the notified maximum?
Registration feeWhat current fee provision applies to this instrument and valuation?
Authority or lease-related chargesDoes the property's allotment or lease require a separate payment or permission?

Copies and subsequent record updatesWhich services are included and which are charged separately?

Online summaries give conflicting figures for registration and processing charges. A reliable estimate should identify the applicable official fee entry and the property details used in the calculation. This article therefore does not present an unverified fixed registration amount as though it follows from the ₹5,000 stamp notification.

Ask for government-payment receipts and keep professional charges separately recorded. If someone quotes a single package price, request the breakdown. This makes it easier to understand what has been paid, what remains to be done and whether mutation or authority formalities are included.

What makes a gift deed legally effective?

Under the ordinary statutory framework, a gift concerns existing property transferred voluntarily without consideration and accepted during the donor's lifetime while the donor remains capable of giving. For an immovable-property gift governed by section 123 of the Transfer of Property Act, the instrument must be registered, signed by or on behalf of the donor and attested by at least two witnesses. Applicable legal exceptions require separate examination. [3]

These requirements deserve as much attention as the concession. The deed should explain the donor's title, precisely identify the interest being gifted and record the intended terms clearly. A reduced stamp amount cannot repair an incorrect property schedule or give the donor an interest that the donor never owned.

Where a parent wishes to retain residence or income, discuss that intention before drafting. Do not rely on an oral family understanding that conflicts with the written deed. The instrument should be examined for how the intended retained rights can lawfully be expressed and how they interact with the transfer.

Do not assume a completed gift can simply be withdrawn because the donor changes their mind. Revocation depends on the applicable law and facts; section 126 addresses limited circumstances, rather than a general right to cancel at will. [3] Anyone concerned about continued control should discuss the difference between a lifetime transfer and a will before choosing the instrument.

Documents to organise before drafting

The exact requisition depends on the property and the parties, but the following starting file usually makes the review more productive:

  • Ownership history: the acquisition deed and earlier papers needed to explain the donor's interest.
  • Property identification: address, plot or gata number, area, boundaries and relevant maps or schedules.
  • Identity details: appropriate identification, address particulars and tax identification where applicable.
  • Relationship evidence: documents supporting the precise family category claimed.
  • Previous gift documents: any earlier gift deed and material showing whether a concession was used.
  • Revenue or municipal records: available current entries and earlier orders relevant to the file.
  • Allotment, lease and permission papers: where the property is subject to an authority or other transfer conditions.
  • Encumbrance and dispute information: mortgage details, pending proceedings, attachments or known competing claims.

Keep originals secure and provide a clearly indexed copy set for initial review. Mark missing records rather than replacing them with assumptions. If there are joint owners, identify each person's share and who is actually proposing to execute the gift. A family member's consent does not, by itself, establish ownership of the entire property.

How to approach gift deed registration in Uttar Pradesh

  1. Define the proposed transfer. Identify the donor, donee, exact property interest and any rights intended to be retained.
  2. Check title and restrictions. Examine the supporting documents and any permission or encumbrance issue.
  3. Assess concession eligibility. Record the family category, property category, ownership capacity and earlier gift history.
  4. Prepare an itemised calculation. Confirm stamp duty and registration charges before execution and payment.
  5. Draft and review the deed. Read names, schedules, acceptance and operative terms carefully.
  6. Complete the official registration procedure. Follow the current IGRSUP application and appointment requirements, with the required parties, witnesses and documents.
  7. Collect and check the registered instrument. Preserve the registration particulars, payment records and complete copy.
  8. Address subsequent record updates. Identify the relevant revenue, municipal or authority process for the property.

The department describes online application and appointment facilities followed by registration at the Sub-Registrar's office. Use its current instructions for the actual submission. Completing an online form alone should not be confused with completing the property transfer. [4]

What should happen after registration?

Read the registered copy promptly. Check that every page and schedule is present and that names, property particulars and shares match the final instructions. Keep an electronic backup as well as the physical records. If a discrepancy appears, obtain advice on the appropriate correction rather than altering the copy yourself.

Next, identify which records should reflect the change. A rural land record, municipal assessment and development-authority record are different systems. The appropriate update depends on the property. Your file should show which applications were made, their acknowledgements and the resulting orders.

For a fuller explanation of that stage, see our guide to mutation and dakhil kharij. Keep the registered gift deed with the supporting title documents; a later record entry should not replace the underlying instrument in your property file.

Does the stamp concession also settle income tax?

No automatic conclusion should be drawn. State stamp treatment and income-tax consequences involve separate provisions. Before a valuable transfer, obtain advice about the recipient's position, any subsequent income from the property and the records needed if it is later sold. A property acquired by gift can raise questions beyond the initial registration expense.

Preserve the earlier owner's acquisition details and relevant improvement records where available. These can be difficult to reconstruct years later. The sensible approach is to arrange the tax review alongside the conveyancing work, rather than assuming that a family relationship makes every future consequence irrelevant.

Frequently asked questions

Can a mother gift property to her married daughter?

Daughter is a listed category without a marital-status qualification in the order examined. Confirm title, relationship evidence and the other conditions for the proposed deed before claiming the concession.

Is a gift to a daughter-in-law covered?

The order identifies the son's wife. Record the relationship accurately and assess the whole transaction, including any previous concessionary gift of the property.

Does the concession apply only to houses?

No. The notified property categories also include agricultural, commercial and industrial property. The donor's title and any independent restrictions still require examination.

Can property belonging to a family company use the concession?

The notification excludes the specified juristic entities and property owned by them. Personal shareholding in a family company should not be confused with personal ownership of its immovable assets.

Does the five-year condition prohibit every resale?

It concerns the concession for an onward gift after an earlier concessionary gift. Do not describe it as a universal five-year prohibition on selling; examine any proposed transaction separately.

Does a gift deed become effective only after the donor dies?

A gift is a lifetime-transfer arrangement. A will serves a different purpose. Before choosing either, discuss when ownership should pass and what rights the owner intends to retain.

Can I assume a fixed ₹6,000 total from an online advertisement?

Ask for the official basis and a written breakup. The ₹5,000 stamp-duty notification alone does not establish that advertised total or determine every additional charge.

Official sources and publication details

  1. UP Government Notification dated 8 January 2026, No. 1/2026/26/94-2-2026-700(29)/2021, including the English translation on page 3.
  2. Stamp and Registration Department: stamp exemptions and incentive policies, official departmental listing.
  3. Transfer of Property Act, 1882, India Code; particularly sections 122, 123 and 126, subject to applicable provisions and exceptions.
  4. IGRSUP official portal and citizen information, for registration facilities and current instructions.
