How to Make a Will in India: Legal Requirements, Registration and Common Mistakes

A practical guide to planning your estate, choosing witnesses and an executor, understanding registration and avoiding preventable disputes.

By H.R. Legal Associate · Published 17 October 2026 · 11 minute read

A will is one of those documents people mean to prepare “when there is time”. Yet a modest estate can leave difficult questions: who should receive the home, what happens to savings, and who will deal with the paperwork? Clear instructions can make those decisions easier for a family already dealing with a loss.

Making a will in India involves more than listing names beside assets. You need to understand what you own, which law applies, how the document should be signed and how it will be found. This guide explains the main questions in plain English, including registration, witnesses and the important December 2025 change concerning probate.

What is a will, and when does it take effect?

A will records how a person intends their property to be dealt with after death. The person making it is the testator; a person receiving a benefit is a beneficiary. An executor is appointed to administer the estate and carry out the instructions, subject to the law and any necessary court process.

A will is different from a lifetime gift. It ordinarily does not transfer ownership immediately, so the maker can continue dealing with their property. This makes it useful for someone who wants to plan succession while retaining control. Whether a will or gift better serves a particular family should be considered before either document is prepared.

Who can make a will in India?

Section 59 of the Indian Succession Act provides the ordinary statutory starting point: an adult of sound mind may dispose of property by will. Capacity concerns understanding the act and its consequences at execution. Age, illness or a disability does not by itself answer that question. Fraud, coercion or pressure that destroys free choice can undermine a will. [1]

Speak with the lawyer yourself wherever possible. Explain your assets, family and wishes without a proposed beneficiary answering everything for you. Where health or capacity may later be disputed, ask whether contemporaneous medical evidence would help. A medical certificate is not a universal substitute for proper execution, nor is it compulsory in every ordinary case.

Does the same will law apply to everyone?

No. Personal law, domicile, property type and special statutory regimes can affect the advice. Do not assume that one online template fits every person across India. Tell the drafter about your religion, family circumstances, residence and property locations, including assets outside India.

Muslim testamentary succession requires separate attention. The extent that may be bequeathed, gifts to heirs and the need for other heirs' consent can differ with the applicable school and circumstances. The commonly discussed one-third limit is assessed against the relevant net estate, not a casual estimate of one asset. Obtain specific advice rather than importing an unrestricted distribution clause from another framework. [6]

Special local regimes, including Goa and Uttarakhand where applicable, and agricultural-tenure provisions may also require examination. This guide describes the ordinary framework; it does not replace an assessment of the law governing a particular estate.

Start with an asset list, then check ownership

Prepare an inventory of land, flats, bank accounts, investments, business interests, jewellery and other significant possessions. Beside each item, note the ownership document, co-owners, approximate value, liabilities and where the records are kept. Separate assets you own from assets you manage for someone else.

For jointly owned property, establish your actual interest. Being the family member who pays bills does not necessarily mean you own the whole asset. A will should not promise a beneficiary a larger interest than the maker can lawfully dispose of. Ancestral or coparcenary interests need separate analysis.

Property descriptions should be specific enough to identify the asset. Use the address, deed particulars and relevant plot, survey or apartment details. If your papers contain inconsistent measurements or names, resolve those issues during drafting. A vague description can move a preventable problem into the administration of the estate.

Describe beneficiaries and shares clearly

Identify beneficiaries by full name and relationship, adding other details where needed to distinguish people with similar names. Explain whether they receive a particular asset, a fractional share or a sum of money. Check that percentages add up and that different clauses do not promise the same property to different people.

Think about alternatives. What should happen if a beneficiary dies before you, refuses the gift or cannot receive it? A suitable substitute provision can address that possibility. Ask how these instructions interact with the applicable succession rules instead of assuming a deceased beneficiary's share always passes in the way you expect.

Include a suitably drafted residuary clause for what remains after specific gifts and expenses. It can address overlooked or later-acquired assets, subject to its wording and applicable law. Without it, an apparently detailed will may still leave part of the estate without an effective testamentary instruction.

Choose an executor who can do the work

The executor's role involves practical responsibility: locating documents, identifying assets, addressing liabilities and arranging distribution. Choose someone reliable who understands the appointment and is willing to act. Consider an alternative if your first choice cannot serve.

A beneficiary may also be an executor; these are different functions. Where there are competing interests, complex businesses or family tensions, discuss whether an independent professional appointment would be more suitable. Clarify expenses and any intended remuneration. A prestigious name is less useful than an executor who can actually administer the estate.

How should an ordinary will be signed and witnessed?

Where section 63 applies, the testator must sign or mark the will, or another person may sign in the testator's presence and at their direction. At least two witnesses must attest in the manner prescribed. Each must see the relevant signing or receive the testator's personal acknowledgement, and sign in the testator's presence. [1]

For a straightforward signing, arrange one organised session with the testator and both witnesses. Although the statute does not require both witnesses to attend simultaneously, doing so can make the circumstances easier to record. Read the complete final document first and use an attestation clause reflecting what actually happened.

Choose independent adult witnesses who can later be identified and contacted. Avoid beneficiaries and their spouses: applicable rules can create problems for benefits given to an attesting witness or spouse. The precise effect requires checking under the governing framework. Keep full names, addresses and contact details with the execution record.

Page numbering, initials and signatures on each page are useful precautions against substitution. They do not cure missing statutory attestation. Make corrections before execution and avoid leaving blanks that someone could later complete.

Is will registration compulsory in India?

Registration of a will is generally optional under section 18 of the Registration Act. Section 27 allows presentation or deposit at any time; wills have special provisions under sections 40–45. An unregistered will is not invalid merely because it is unregistered, provided the applicable requirements are met. [2]

Registration can provide an additional official record, but it does not guarantee that the document will survive every challenge. It does not automatically establish capacity, remove suspicious circumstances or cure defective execution. The Supreme Court has repeatedly distinguished registration from the proof required for a will. [5]

If you choose registration, check the relevant office's current appointment, identification and fee requirements. Do not assume one state's fee or procedure applies everywhere. Registration, notarisation and probate perform different functions and should not be described as interchangeable certificates of validity.

Does a will need stamp paper or notarisation?

An ordinary will can be prepared on plain paper; stamp paper does not make it stronger. Official Delhi registration guidance, for example, states that no stamp duty is required for registration of a will. Registration fees and other service charges are separate matters. [7]

Notarisation is not a replacement for the witnessing required under the applicable will law. A notary's seal should not distract from the actual signing process. Likewise, handwritten and typed documents should be examined for legal execution and clear intention rather than judged solely by their appearance.

Probate in 2026: the important legal update

Probate is a court grant concerning a will and the executor's authority. Where no executor can act, letters of administration with the will annexed may be relevant. These are different from registering the document before a Sub-Registrar.

The Repealing and Amending Act, 2025, Act 37 of 2025, omitted section 213 of the Indian Succession Act. Older advice presenting that section's territorial and community-based probate requirement as current law therefore needs updating. The amendment did not abolish probate jurisdiction or make every disputed will self-proving. [3]

After a death, assess the estate, any contest, pending proceedings and the requirements of the institutions holding assets. A probate or administration application may still be appropriate. Ask what legal basis supports a demand for a grant, rather than assuming either that probate is always compulsory or that it can never be needed.

How do nominations fit into a will?

Keep nominations, joint-holding arrangements and testamentary instructions under review together. They can serve different purposes. A nominee's entitlement depends on the statute or scheme governing the asset; nomination should not be treated as a universal method of overriding succession. Recent Supreme Court discussion confirms the importance of examining the governing law. [8]

Prepare an asset-by-asset note rather than one blanket conclusion for all accounts, shares and insurance. Ask whether the institution needs updated nominations or additional documents. Consistent records can reduce confusion even where the ultimate legal entitlement must be determined separately.

Minor children, dependants and practical arrangements

If a beneficiary is a minor, think about who will manage the intended benefit and how expenses will be met. A simple direction to “give everything to my child” may leave important administration questions unresolved. Trust arrangements, executor powers and guardianship wishes need careful, individual drafting.

Where a spouse or dependant should continue living in a home, explain that objective precisely. Discuss occupation, expenses, later sale and the ultimate destination of the property. Instructions about personal belongings, pets or sentimental objects can also prevent disagreements, but should be realistic and consistent with the main provisions.

Can you change or revoke your will?

A will can ordinarily be changed while the maker remains competent, following the applicable legal method. A codicil is a supplementary testamentary document, but several amendments can become difficult to read together. For substantial changes, a fresh coordinated will is often easier to administer. [1]

Ask the drafter to address earlier wills and codicils expressly and preserve a clear record of the operative version. Do not casually cross out clauses or replace a page after signing. If an earlier document was registered, discuss how the new instructions and records should be handled.

Review the plan after marriage, divorce, a birth, a death, a major purchase or a move abroad. The effect of marriage and other events varies under the applicable law. Multiple-country wills require coordination so that one document does not unintentionally revoke another.

Can an online or video will replace a signed document?

An online service can help prepare a draft, but drafting on a screen and legally executing a will are different steps. The Information Technology Act's First Schedule excludes wills from its ordinary electronic-recognition framework. Do not assume an electronic signature, email or recorded video alone creates a valid ordinary will. [4]

A video of a planned execution may provide supplementary context, but it should not be advertised as a substitute for the required formalities. Store digital copies securely and avoid placing passwords or access codes directly in a document that may later be disclosed.

Where should the original be kept?

Choose storage that protects the original and allows it to be found. Tell the executor or another trusted person where it is and how access can lawfully be obtained. A perfectly drafted will hidden beyond anyone's knowledge can create a practical obstacle after death.

Keep a separate, updated asset inventory and document-location list. Sensitive account references and access instructions can be managed securely without continually rewriting dispositive clauses. Ensure that a locked storage arrangement does not depend entirely on the maker being alive to open it.

Common mistakes worth avoiding

  • Using a template without checking personal law or ownership.
  • Describing beneficiaries or property too vaguely.
  • Ignoring debts, substitute beneficiaries and the residuary estate.
  • Letting an interested person control the entire preparation.
  • Confusing registration or notarisation with proper execution.
  • Keeping contradictory originals without explaining which governs.
  • Assuming nominations resolve every succession question.
  • Leaving the original inaccessible to the executor.

These are practical drafting risks, not predictions that every imperfect document will fail. The aim is to make intention clear and preserve evidence of a free, informed decision.

What to bring to a will-drafting consultation

Bring identification, a family chart, ownership papers, a private asset list and details of liabilities. Add existing wills, codicils, nominations and any relevant orders or family arrangements. Write your wishes in ordinary language first; the lawyer can then identify legal issues and convert those wishes into workable clauses.

For property in Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh or another state, mention its actual location and tenure. For overseas assets, provide the jurisdiction and existing arrangements. A useful consultation ends with identified decisions and missing information, rather than a signature on a document nobody has fully reviewed.

Frequently asked questions

Can I write my own will?

Professional drafting is not universally mandatory. However, legal review is valuable where ownership, personal law, dependants or cross-border assets make the instructions more complicated.

Does registration prevent a challenge?

No. Registration can help preserve a record, but the will may still require proof and examination of the circumstances in which it was made.

Should I wait until retirement?

Planning can be useful whenever you have assets or responsibilities. Review and update the arrangements as circumstances change rather than treating the first document as permanent.

Must every will now go through probate?

No blanket answer applies to every estate. Section 213 was omitted in December 2025, while probate and administration remain available legal processes. Seek current advice about the particular situation.
