How to Check Property Registry and Obtain a 12-Year Encumbrance Certificate in UP

Plan a clear registration search with correct property identifiers, a defined 12-year period and detailed follow-up checks for mortgages and title history.

What an IGRSUP barah sala certificate tells you

A barah sala search is commonly understood as a search of registration records over a twelve-year period. Buyers use the expression when asking about encumbrance information and the history of registered transactions affecting a property. The useful result is a record-specific search whose property, office, period and limitations are clearly understood.

A registration search can help identify registered instruments such as transfers or security documents within its actual scope. It is not a government guarantee that no claim of any kind exists. Unregistered interests, some forms of security, possession disputes, litigation and statutory issues may require separate investigation.

2026 service note: Use the current services and instructions of IGRSUP, the UP Stamp and Registration Department, or the competent registration office. This guide does not assume that every office, historical period or record is fully searchable online, or that every search service uses the same certificate name and output.

The central task is to request the correct property search, obtain the underlying instruments where needed and interpret the result as part of title verification.

Why the exact search period matters

“Twelve years” must be converted into actual dates. A request covering a defined start date and end date is more precise than an instruction to “check the last few years”. The dates should fit the proposed transaction and the service's supported search method.

A twelve-year registration search is not automatically a complete title investigation. The root of title may be older, and a material deed, inheritance, lease or restriction outside the window can still affect the property. Select the broader title review period according to the property's history and purpose.

A search also needs a sufficiently current end date. If there is a long gap between the search and completion, investigate relevant intervening transactions through the available official process and other appropriate checks. A clean old result does not establish that nothing has happened since.

How to check property registry records through IGRSUP

Begin at the official departmental website. Identify the available service for document search, property information, certified copy or the defined search certificate you need. Confirm the office, period and identifiers supported by that service. Avoid assuming that a private website's menu names describe the current official process.

Enter the particulars from the title documents and retain the search or application reference. Where a payment is required, use the authorised payment route and preserve the acknowledgement. A successful payment should be reconciled with the actual application and delivered result.

If online results are incomplete or the required record is not available there, identify the competent registration office and prescribed record-search or copy procedure. Do not interpret an unavailable digital result as a confirmed absence of registered transactions.

How to read search entries and obtain the right copies

An entry may show enough information to identify an instrument but not enough to understand its legal effect. Obtain the full document when it affects ownership, security, shares, authority or the property description. Read the schedules and endorsements, not merely the document label.

A mortgage entry requires examination of the security, property covered, lender and discharge arrangements. A later transfer requires comparison with the seller's claimed interest. A cancellation, release or rectification must be read for its actual scope; its title alone does not establish that every earlier issue disappeared.

What a nil or clear result actually means

A nil result should be understood within the search's identifiers, period, office and record coverage. It may be useful evidence that the search did not reveal the relevant recorded instruments. It does not mean the property is free from every possible legal, financial or possession issue.

Check whether the search used historical numbers, parent parcels and relevant name variations. A parcel created from a larger holding may have transactions recorded under the parent description. A mechanically narrow search can miss the context needed to interpret the present plot.

Retain the official output and the search inputs together. A PDF saying “no entry” is much less useful if the property description and dates used cannot be established later.

Mortgages, original deeds and lender release

Ask where the original title documents are held and why. If they are with a lender, obtain the loan and security details and a documented release arrangement. If the seller says the loan is closed, seek appropriate closure and release evidence rather than relying on a general bank statement.

Not every relevant security position is fully answered by one registration search. Depending on the property and transaction, other official security searches and direct lender verification may be relevant. Such searches have their own scope and should not be described as universal title certificates.

Coordinate the purchase payment, lender discharge and handover of originals. The agreement should identify who receives money, the conditions for release and the evidence required at completion. Paying the full seller balance before the security arrangement is settled can leave an avoidable gap.

An example: a plot carved from a larger holding

Assume a buyer is offered plot B-18 in a private layout. The seller provides a recent deed describing an interest in a larger khasra holding, while the layout uses its own plot numbering. Searching only “B-18” may not connect with the registered property description.

The search should reconcile the private plot, parent parcel, ownership chain and approved layout where applicable. Obtain the instrument by which the seller acquired the relevant interest and any later subdivision or transfer documents. Check security affecting the larger holding, not only the small plot name used in the brochure.

The output should explain how the plot derives from the parent land, which records were searched and what remains unresolved. That is a meaningful title-search result; a generic “barah sala clear” statement without identifiers is not.

Fees, application tracking and final documents

Government search and copy charges depend on the current service and applicable schedule. Ask for an itemised statement separating official charges from professional search, analysis and coordination fees. Preserve the official receipts and references even where a professional handles the application.

Track the application to the delivered record. If the result contains the wrong period, office or property, use the correction or fresh-request route appropriate to the error. A receipt for a search does not establish that the correct search has been completed.

Refresh the search where the deal changes before completion

A search relates to a defined property and period. If the seller changes the offered area, adds an adjoining parcel or proposes a different ownership structure, compare the revised transaction with the original request. A certificate for one parcel should not silently become the search evidence for another.

The same applies where the seller introduces a new representative or reports that a lender has been paid. Obtain the supporting authority or release material and assess whether the registration search or other enquiries need updating.

Maintain the earlier result as part of the chronology. A later update supplements or changes the analysis; it should not erase what was searched previously. The complete file should explain why the final transaction is covered by the records relied on.

This approach is useful for both buyers and lenders. It turns “search completed” into a specific statement about the property, dates and evidence, and makes the remaining completion work visible before funds are released.

Frequently asked questions

Is twelve years always enough to establish title?

No. It defines the requested search period, not a universal rule for every property's ownership history. The root and continuity of title may require earlier instruments and other records.

Does a nil search mean there is no mortgage?

Interpret it within the actual search scope. Examine original-document custody, lender information and other relevant security records. A nil result is not a guarantee covering every security arrangement.

Can I use a deed index instead of the complete deed?

An index helps locate a document. The full instrument is needed to understand the rights, conditions, property schedule and legal effect of a material transaction.

Should the search be updated before registry?

Consider the gap between the search end date and completion, the property's history and the available official process. The completion file should explain how relevant intervening events have been addressed.
