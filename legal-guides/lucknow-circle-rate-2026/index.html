Lucknow Circle Rate 2026: Area-Wise Property Rates and How to Check Them

Find the right locality entry and valuation category, understand area and road factors, and use a detailed worksheet before a Lucknow property registry.

What is the Lucknow circle rate?

The Lucknow circle rate is the commonly used name for the collector's minimum valuation schedule relevant to property assessment for stamp purposes. It is not a guarantee of the property's market price, a lending valuation or a certificate that a development is approved. Its practical role is to help identify the value on which the transaction is assessed under the applicable rules.

A buyer searching for “Lucknow circle rate 2026” usually needs one number. The correct answer starts with a more precise question: which property, which location entry, which use, which area unit and which effective schedule? A citywide average cannot reliably answer a registration question.

2026 schedule note: This article does not reproduce unverified current rupee-per-square-metre figures. Obtain the effective locality schedule and amendments from the Lucknow district administration or the UP registration department. The locality table below is a lookup guide, not a notified rate chart.

The aim is to help you reach the correct entry and understand the resulting calculation. An old PDF with “Lucknow” in its title is not enough unless its effective period and property category match your transaction.

Area-wise Lucknow lookup: what to identify first

Familiar neighbourhood names are helpful for a property search but can be too broad for valuation. A property advertised as Shaheed Path, Sultanpur Road or Faizabad Road may fall in a specific village, colony, road segment or authority scheme. The deed and jurisdiction records are more reliable starting points than the listing description.

Use the following table to organise the lookup. It intentionally supplies identification tasks rather than invented area rates. The correct notified figure is the output of those tasks.

Step-by-step: how to check the applicable circle rate

Start at the official district website or IGRSUP, using the departmental or district service links available there. Look for the collector rate list, valuation list, minimum valuation schedule or related stamp valuation notice. The label and navigation can change, so identify the document by its issuing authority and content rather than a screenshot from an old tutorial.

Record the publication or notification details and effective date. Check for later amendments, corrigenda and separate schedules covering particular jurisdictions or categories. Saving only the page containing a rupee figure can omit the conditions that explain how that figure applies.

Then identify the property entry. Match the deed's location description and jurisdiction to the schedule's headings. Read any general instructions before multiplying area by rate. These may address construction, road width, corner positions, mixed use or other factors.

Finally, reproduce the calculation in writing. A reliable file contains the source document, relevant entry, unit conversions, adjustments, comparison with consideration and the assessment used for payment. This lets a second reader check the result without repeating the entire search.

How to calculate a basic land valuation

For a simple illustration, assume a plot area of 150 square metres and an assumed schedule rate of ₹40,000 per square metre. Multiplying those figures produces an assumed land value of ₹60,00,000. Neither the area rate nor the result represents a notified 2026 rate for a named Lucknow locality.

If the relevant valuation instructions require additions or adjustments, apply them before treating the result as the assessable value. For a built property, assess whether the schedule separately values construction. For an apartment, use the method applicable to that property category rather than mechanically treating it as a vacant plot.

Road frontage, commercial use and construction

Road-facing land can be valued differently from interior property where the schedule makes that distinction. Establish actual access and the scheduled road classification. A broker's description of a proposed future road does not prove that the present entry or adjustment applies.

Mixed-use premises also require careful classification. A shop on the ground floor with residential use above it may involve more than one valuation component. Record the actual configuration and examine the schedule rather than selecting whichever single category produces the lowest figure.

For construction, keep a plan, area statement and property description that can be reconciled with the deed. If the building extends beyond the sanctioned plan, that creates an approval issue as well as a potential discrepancy in the description. Paying duty on a structure does not regularise it.

Flats, authority properties and agricultural land

An apartment purchase brings together the unit, common areas, undivided land interest and project documents. The schedule's apartment method may not match a vacant-plot calculation. Check the unit identification, super or covered area terminology and the basis actually used in the applicable rules.

For an LDA or other authority property, examine the allotment, lease or conveyance, transfer conditions and outstanding dues alongside valuation. A circle-rate calculation does not eliminate a lease restriction or replace a transfer permission. Read our LDA-approved plot and layout guide for the approval checks.

For agricultural land, confirm the revenue village, parcel identity, area unit and tenure. Intended residential use is a separate question. Our Section 80 land conversion guide explains why revenue conversion and planning approval need separate examination.

What to do when two sources show different rates

First compare the effective dates, jurisdictions and categories. Many apparent conflicts disappear once an old chart is separated from a later amendment or a broad locality is separated from a specific road entry. A property website's estimate is not equivalent to the issued schedule.

If the official materials remain ambiguous, present the property description and the competing entries to the competent registration or valuation office. Seek a documented clarification or assessment through the applicable process. This is more useful than selecting the lower number and hoping it will pass.

Do not alter the property's description to make it fit a preferred rate. The deed must accurately identify what is sold. An incorrect description can create title, registration and valuation difficulties that cost far more than the apparent saving.

Build an area-wise comparison without mixing valuation entries

A buyer comparing three Lucknow plots can create a useful shortlist without pretending one headline rate describes an entire neighbourhood. Put each property on its own line and record its exact revenue or scheme identity, schedule entry, unit and effective date. Add the quoted price and intended use in separate columns.

This is especially useful along expanding road corridors, where several villages and private layouts may be marketed under one familiar location name. A lower assumed rate can disappear once the correct road entry, construction component or land category is identified.

A practical comparison sheet for two shortlisted plots
Comparison fieldProperty A fileProperty B file
Legal locationEnter the actual village or scheme, jurisdiction and parcel or plot identifier.Use the same level of detail; a road name alone is not a comparable location description.
Schedule sourceRecord the authority, notification, effective date and page.Check whether the same effective schedule applies or whether a different jurisdictional entry governs.

The completed sheet lets you ask precise questions of the registration or valuation office. Instead of “What is the rate near Shaheed Path?”, you can identify the parcel and ask which scheduled entry and instructions govern it.

It also improves the purchase agreement. If the final assessment is still being clarified, document how the government charges will be determined and who bears them. The contract should not rest on an intermediary's unverified estimate presented as a fixed official demand.

Use the comparison as a decision aid, not as a substitute for the official assessment. Its value is consistency: the same facts and method are applied to each property, and every important number has an identifiable source.

Frequently asked questions about Lucknow circle rates

Is one rate applicable to all of Gomti Nagar?

Do not assume so. Identify the exact location, property category and schedule entry. Sector, road, use and property type can matter under the issued valuation framework.

Can I buy below the circle rate?

The negotiated price and stamp assessment are separate questions. A lower price does not itself decide the assessable value, and a material difference can raise additional legal or tax questions requiring its own analysis.

Does a high circle rate mean the plot is LDA approved?

No. Valuation and planning approval perform different functions. Verify the sanctioned layout, plot identity, land use and approval conditions independently through the responsible authority.

Should I keep the rate-list PDF after registry?

Yes. Save the relevant schedule and instructions, the calculation and the payment references with the registered deed. They form a useful record of the assessment basis and help answer later questions about the transaction.

Official links and the final valuation file

Use the Lucknow district website for district notices and official service gateways and IGRSUP for registration-related valuation services and departmental material. Read the stamp duty and registration cost guide to turn the assessed value into a complete transaction budget.

A sound valuation file ties one identifiable property to one effective schedule, the correct unit, documented adjustments and an explainable calculation. That is the practical answer to a circle-rate search: a source-backed assessment you can use at registration, rather than a citywide number with no connection to the land being purchased.
