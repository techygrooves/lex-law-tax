A plot buying legal checklist for Lucknow

Buying a plot in Lucknow involves more than selecting a location and comparing a price per square foot. The buyer acquires a defined legal interest in land, subject to its title, tenure, approvals, access and transaction terms. A plot shown by an agent must be connected to the property the seller can legally convey.

Start with five workstreams: identify the plot, understand the interest offered, verify title and claims, check development permissions, and plan registry and handover. Freehold, leasehold, LDA and RERA are important terms, but none should be used as a shortcut that replaces the other checks.

This 2026 checklist explains the decision process without naming unverified approved projects or claiming live local rates. Use current records from LDA, UP RERA, IGRSUP and UP Bhulekh for the relevant parts of the file.

The practical output is a purchase-ready record showing exactly what is being bought, from whom, under which approvals, at what complete cost and with what possession and documents delivered.

Freehold and leasehold: understand the interest first

Freehold ownership and leasehold rights are different legal interests. A freehold title still remains subject to applicable law, planning controls, encumbrances and rights affecting the land. Leasehold property is held under a lease with its term, permitted use, transfer conditions and other covenants.

Do not assume that a familiar authority name means every property is freehold, or that the seller's use of the word “owner” answers the lease position. Read the actual lease, conveyance, conversion document and subsequent instruments.

Identify the plot across every record

Write the district, jurisdiction, village or scheme, khasra or gata, layout plot number, dimensions, area and boundaries. For private plotting, connect the small plot to the parent holding. A project map with attractive numbering is not itself the legal link.

Compare the deed, revenue entry, sanctioned layout and site. If one document describes an undivided share while another promises a separate plot, resolve the legal form before agreeing to buy. Area and location are both important; an equal area elsewhere in the holding is not necessarily the roadside plot shown.

Trace title and all necessary ownership interests

Read how the present seller acquired the plot and examine the material earlier links. A deed can be registered while the transferor lacked the entire interest purportedly conveyed. Identify succession, partition, release, authority allotment and development arrangements where they appear in the chain.

For inherited or jointly held land, establish the actual shares and participation required. A single recorded holder or salesperson may not be able to convey the whole property. Where an attorney acts, verify authority separately from ownership.

Our Lucknow title verification guide explains the report structure. The buyer should receive findings tied to the exact property, documents examined, search scope and completion conditions.

Search for mortgages, earlier transfers and disputes

Use relevant registration searches and obtain the underlying instruments where needed. Identify the property, office, period and historical descriptions searched. A defined-period result is useful within its scope; it is not a guarantee against every unregistered or public-law claim.

Ask who holds the original documents and why. If a lender has security, obtain a documented release arrangement for the property or plot. A promoter's receipt does not independently release a mortgage over the parent land.

LDA approval and private layout checks

Identify the competent planning authority and obtain the actual approval claimed. An LDA-allotted plot is different from a private layout sanctioned by LDA. Each requires the documents supporting its own title and transfer route.

Read the complete order, land schedule, drawing and important conditions. Match the plot against roads, parks, facilities and the approved boundary. A genuine first-phase approval should not be extended to later land without the operative revision or other relevant permission.

Use our LDA approved plots guide for the detailed sanction comparison. Registry, tax assessment and a nearby approved colony do not substitute for approval of the plot offered.

RERA project and phase checks

Where the plotted development falls within the registration framework, locate it on the official UP RERA service. Compare registration number, promoter, phase, land and the offered plot. A marketing brand may cover more than one phase or legal entity.

Read relevant disclosures, approvals and orders rather than treating a number as a quality badge. Match the agreement and payment demands to the verified project. The RERA verification checklist explains the buyer file in detail.

Agricultural land, Section 80 and a house plan

A plot can be marketed for a house while remaining part of an agricultural holding. Examine tenure, transferability, subdivision and the intended-use route. A registered agricultural share is not automatically an exclusively owned residential site.

A Section 80 declaration, where relevant, must be checked against the exact land, area and use. It has a revenue function; planning and building permissions require separate analysis. Our agricultural land residential-use guide explains the full sequence.

Before buying for construction, test the intended house against plot dimensions, access and applicable building requirements. A low land price is less useful if the legally usable site cannot support the building the buyer wants.

Roads, access and services

Check the legal basis and physical route of access. The plot may be near a main road without holding a lawful connection to it. A private approach needs a legally sufficient basis, and the sanctioned layout should be compared with the site.

Separate existing amenities from promised development. Identify what roads, drainage, electricity, water and common facilities are actually available, what permissions or works are outstanding and who must provide them. Record the obligations in the agreement.

Budget beyond the advertised price

The total purchase cost can include stamp duty, registration fee, authority dues, finance expenses, record searches and professional work. Identify which costs are government charges, which arise from the property documents and which are agreed services.

Use the effective valuation schedule and instrument assessment. Do not treat a price per square foot as the stamp calculation or assume a concession without its current notification. Read the UP stamp duty guide and women's concession guide for the relevant worksheets.

Agreement, registry and possession

Make the agreement correspond to the verified plot. State the price, payment milestones, documents, approvals, lender release and possession obligations. Missing evidence should become a specific condition rather than an undefined promise to provide papers later.

Review the final deed before execution. Confirm shares, property identity, consideration, authority and the statement of possession. At handover, record keys or access, occupation position and originals delivered. After registry, complete the relevant revenue, authority, municipal and utility updates.

Example: freehold label versus a lease document

Assume a seller describes a plot as freehold but supplies an authority lease and no later conveyance. The buyer should establish whether a lawful conversion or other instrument changed the interest. The sales description alone does not do so.

Organise the purchase decision by unresolved workstream

A buyer can make the checklist easier to use by assigning each unresolved item to a document, responsible person and completion event. Avoid an unexplained numerical score that suggests a mortgage, missing owner and unreadable copy are interchangeable defects. Their legal consequences are different.

Some issues can be resolved by obtaining a missing record; others require a legal act or a changed transaction. The report should explain which is which. A pending approval should not be called a missing photocopy, and an absent owner should not be treated as a minor spelling error.

Put the same register into the agreement process. If the seller must obtain a release or demonstrate a layout match, identify the milestone before the relevant payment. That makes the due-diligence result part of the transaction rather than a document filed away after booking.

The buyer should also state the intended use. A plot suitable for long-term landholding may not meet the same practical needs as a site intended for immediate house construction or commercial activity. Title is essential in each case, while the required use, access and building evidence follows the actual plan.

At final review, ask whether another professional could understand the purchase from the file without relying on the salesperson's memory. The answer should be visible in the title chain, approvals, site identification, cost sheet and closing terms.

This method keeps the process practical and human-readable. It replaces a pile of unchecked documents with a set of specific questions, answers and actions that explain what the buyer will acquire and when the purchase is ready to complete.

Frequently asked questions

Is freehold always free from restrictions?

No. Planning laws, encumbrances, access rights and other legal constraints can affect freehold property. Examine them separately from the nature of the title.

Does RERA replace an LDA layout check?

No. Project registration and planning sanction have different functions. Match the plot, land and phase across both where applicable.

Should I pay a booking amount before seeing the documents?

Establish the basic seller, property and approval file and understand the booking terms first. Substantial unconditional payments reduce the buyer's ability to require missing evidence before completion.

What is the strongest purchase-ready checklist?

One that identifies the exact property, legal interest, owner, claims, approvals, access, full cost and completion deliverables, with unresolved issues assigned specific actions.

Official sources and related reading

Use LDA, UP RERA, IGRSUP and UP Bhulekh for their respective records. For local corridor questions, read the BKT and Sitapur Road registry guide.

A complete plot purchase combines title, approval and ground evidence with a precise contract. That gives the buyer a defined legal interest and a workable completion plan instead of relying on a cluster of attractive labels.
