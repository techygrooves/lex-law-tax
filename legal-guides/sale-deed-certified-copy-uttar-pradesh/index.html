How to Get a Certified Copy of a Sale Deed in Uttar Pradesh

Locate the registered instrument, prepare a precise copy request and check the delivered deed for completeness, authenticity and the correct property.

What a certified sale deed copy provides

A certified copy of a sale deed reproduces the registered instrument through the competent registration record process. It is useful for title investigation, a missing working copy, litigation, inheritance and other documentary needs. The copy should identify the deed, its parties, property, schedules and official registration material relevant to the record.

The service is different from downloading a revenue extract or asking a deed writer to recreate an old draft. A khatauni is a revenue record; a draft shows proposed wording; the registered deed records the instrument actually registered. Request the correct record for the question you need to answer.

Use IGRSUP, the UP Stamp and Registration Department and the competent registration office to identify the current copy service. This 2026 guide does not claim that every historical deed is immediately downloadable online or that a fixed fee and turnaround apply to every request.

Start with registration particulars, then verify the delivered document. A certified copy of a similarly named person's deed or another portion of the land is still the wrong record for your transaction.

Information needed to locate the deed

Collect the registration office, year, document number and any other available registration particulars. Add the parties' names, date of execution or registration, property description and relevant village or locality. An earlier scan, lender file, tax record or previous title report may supply useful references.

If some details are unknown, begin with the official search route supported for the record and period. Use the known property and party information to identify candidate instruments, then compare them carefully. A common name alone is not a reliable match.

How to request the certified copy

Open the official department website and identify the relevant search or copy service. Follow the current instructions for the record, office and period. Where the required record is not available online, use the competent office's prescribed application procedure.

Enter the registration particulars and property details from reliable documents. Preserve the acknowledgement and reference. If payment is required, use the authorised route and reconcile the official receipt with the bank transaction. Professional assistance charges should be separately agreed and identified.

Track the request to the actual document delivered. An application receipt proves filing or payment, not that the correct full copy has been obtained. Record any unavailable record or missing page and the follow-up taken.

What to do when the registration number is missing

Search the available official index using the supported particulars and a reasoned date range. A purchaser's name, seller's name, location and approximate acquisition period can help narrow the inquiry. Keep a note of what was searched and why a particular result was selected.

Where the property has changed identifiers or jurisdiction, compare older deeds and revenue records. A subsequent deed may recite the earlier registration particulars. Such a recital is a lead to the source record, not a substitute for obtaining and examining it.

Check the delivered certified copy page by page

Read the parties, property and registration particulars first. Then examine the operative transfer, consideration, rights, conditions and schedules. The complete instrument can contain restrictions or references that are absent from an index entry or a seller's summary.

Check page continuity, legibility, annexures and authentication. Where the service provides electronic verification, use the official method for that document. A stamp image or filename saying “certified” is not itself proof of official certification.

When the original sale deed is lost

First establish whether the original is genuinely lost, retained by a lender, held by a co-owner or otherwise in known custody. A seller's statement of loss should be checked against the transaction history and security position. Original-document custody can be material to a purchase or mortgage inquiry.

Preserve the facts of the loss and follow the appropriate reporting and documentary steps for the intended use. A bank, purchaser or court may require particular evidence. There is no universal private affidavit that automatically makes every lost-original problem disappear.

A certified copy helps reconstruct and examine the registered record. It does not, merely by being issued, prove that no security exists or that the original cannot be held by another person. The title and encumbrance inquiry remains necessary.

Lender custody is different from loss

If a lender holds originals, obtain the loan and security information and the documented release arrangement. The buyer should know what amount must be paid, who receives it and what evidence establishes discharge and return of documents.

Do not describe lender-held originals as lost to avoid that completion work. The agreement and deed should accurately reflect the security and payment sequence. A certified copy allows reading of the instrument but does not substitute for a required lender release.

Certified copies in a title search

Obtain material earlier deeds where the seller's current document recites them or where the ownership chain needs verification. Compare the property and rights through each link. A registered instrument may transfer less than the whole property or contain a continuing condition.

A copy can also reveal a mismatch between a private scan and the official record. Preserve both versions and investigate through the competent process. Do not alter the certified text to match the preferred draft or revenue entry.

Example: obtaining an inherited owner's old deed

An heir may know the house address but not the deceased owner's registration number. Start with the available title file, lender records and subsequent documents referring to the acquisition. Use the relevant official search to identify the likely instrument, then compare parties and property before ordering the full copy.

The certified deed answers how the earlier owner acquired the property. It does not by itself decide who inherited afterward. That requires the separate succession and share analysis described in our inherited property transfer guide.

What if the certified deed differs from the seller's photocopy?

Preserve both versions and identify the precise difference. A missing schedule, unreadable page, omitted endorsement and a changed operative clause are not the same problem. First establish whether the issued copy is complete and corresponds to the correct instrument and record.

If the discrepancy is material, obtain clarification or the appropriate complete record through the issuing office. Do not reconcile conflicting text by selecting the version that best supports the proposed sale. The buyer's report should identify the source and significance of the difference.

A genuine registered rectification can explain a later description, but it should be obtained and read alongside the original. Its effect may be limited to a particular correction. It should not be treated as a blanket validation of unrelated ownership or authority issues.

If the source record itself appears incorrect or authenticity is disputed, identify the legal remedy and competent authority for that problem. The copy service reproduces records; it does not necessarily adjudicate the underlying title or execution dispute. A separate civil or other proceeding may be relevant depending on the facts.

For routine professional use, label the files accurately: private scan, official certified copy, original inspected, or later rectification. Avoid a folder in which every version is named “final deed”. An indexed chronology makes it much easier to understand the actual documentary position.

Before completion, the agreement and proposed deed should use the description supported by the operative title documents. A mismatch left unexplained can return during mutation, finance, resale or litigation. Obtaining the right copy is therefore part of a legal investigation, not merely a printing task.

Retain the verification note with the copy so that a later reader understands why a difference was accepted as explained or remained an unresolved condition.

Frequently asked questions

Is a certified copy the same as the original?

It is an officially certified reproduction, with the legal and evidentiary use applicable to it. Original existence and custody remain separate factual questions that can matter in a transaction.

Can Bhulekh provide a certified sale deed?

Bhulekh concerns revenue records. Obtain the registered instrument through the registration record system and use revenue extracts for their separate purpose.

Can I get a copy without the document number?

Begin with the official search supported for the available names, property, office and period. Identify and verify the instrument before requesting the copy.

Does a copy prove the property is free from a mortgage?

No. Examine security, lender information, original-document custody and relevant searches. The copy reproduces the instrument; it does not certify the absence of every encumbrance.

Official links and a complete copy file

Use IGRSUP and the competent registration office for registered instruments, the Lucknow district website for official local gateways and UP Bhulekh for separate revenue records.

Keep the search note, application, fee acknowledgement, delivered copy, verification and the reason it was obtained. That organised file makes the certified copy useful for a purchase, inheritance, loan or legal proceeding and shows exactly which registered record has been examined.
