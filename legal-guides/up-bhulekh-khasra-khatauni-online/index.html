UP Bhulekh khasra khatauni: what the records tell you

UP Bhulekh is the official land-record service used to access relevant Uttar Pradesh revenue records. For a landowner or buyer, it is a practical starting point for identifying a holding, recorded tenure holders, parcel references and other entries. The record becomes much more useful when you understand what each field means and connect it to the underlying documents.

A khatauni is a revenue record relating to holdings and tenure holders under the applicable system. A khasra or gata reference helps identify the relevant field or parcel. A khata or account reference groups the recorded holding information. The terms should be read as used in the actual record; they are not interchangeable labels for a registered sale deed.

2026 service note: Use UP Bhulekh through the official government service. This guide explains the record-search method and interpretation without claiming a newly launched interface, guaranteed update speed or universal availability of every historical record online.

An online entry is an important piece of the investigation. It is not a conclusive title certificate, a sanctioned layout or proof that the physical plot shown by a seller matches the revenue parcel.

Information to collect before searching

Write down the district, tehsil, revenue village and any known khasra, gata or khata number. Obtain these from an earlier khatauni, acquisition deed, mutation order or other reliable document. A property advertisement often omits the identifiers needed for a meaningful search.

If searching by a recorded person's name is available, collect the accurate name and identifying context. Similar names, spelling variations and transliterations can produce several possible matches. A name match alone is not enough to identify the land offered for sale.

Record the purpose of the search: a purchase, inheritance, partition, loan, correction or general record update. That purpose determines whether a current view is sufficient or whether certified records, historical entries and underlying orders are needed.

How to search the official UP Bhulekh service

Start at the official UP Bhulekh website or a service link supplied by the government district or revenue department. Select the relevant record service and location in the sequence the current interface provides. Screens and menu names can change, so follow the actual service rather than an old sequence of screenshots.

Choose the supported search method and enter the known identifier accurately. If the result is a list, compare the village, parcel, account and recorded holder information before opening the record. Do not select the first similar-looking name and treat it as a confirmed match.

When the record appears, save or note its source, search date, location, identifiers and record period. If the service distinguishes an ordinary viewing copy from an authenticated or certified record, retain that distinction in your file. Printing a screen does not change its legal status.

How to read a khatauni without overreading it

Begin with the heading: jurisdiction, village and record period. Then read the recorded holder information and the parcels associated with the account. Check the area unit and any share or tenure description. A large holding appearing under several names should not be read as exclusive ownership by the person who happens to be negotiating the sale.

Read remarks and order references carefully. They can point to a transfer, succession, dispute, restriction or administrative change that needs further examination. The short entry is not the full order; obtain the operative document where the issue affects the proposed transaction.

Compare Bhulekh with the registered deed

The deed explains the transaction and rights transferred; the revenue record shows the administrative entry. Compare the seller, acquisition route, parcel references, area and shares across both. A difference is a question to resolve, not an automatic conclusion that one document is false.

An entry may lag behind a transfer or contain an error. Equally, a seller may be relying on a mutation entry without a sound underlying title. Obtain the supporting deed or order and establish the sequence before deciding what the discrepancy means.

For a plotted development, connect the small plot number in the layout to the larger revenue parcels. A plot called “A-12” may not appear as a separate khasra entry. The promoter must show the lawful subdivision, title and approvals linking that marketed plot to the underlying land.

Use maps to identify the land on the ground

A record search should be followed by a spatial check where the land's location or boundary matters. Use the applicable official map service, available through the revenue system or UP BhuNaksha, where the relevant area and record are supported.

Compare neighbouring parcel references, access and the general shape with the title schedule and the site. A map view does not replace a lawful demarcation or professional measurement when a boundary is disputed. Screen scale, historical survey information and ground changes can all affect interpretation.

Where a seller shows a road, verify the legal basis of that access. A line on a private sketch does not prove ownership of the route or a legally enforceable right to use it. Access should be investigated as part of the parcel file, not left until construction begins.

Mutation and correction after a sale or inheritance

Mutation is the process for updating relevant revenue entries following the event recognised under the applicable law. For a sale, retain the registered instrument and the mutation acknowledgement or proceeding record. For inheritance, the succession facts and documents must support the persons and shares entered.

A correction request and a contested ownership dispute are different matters. A clerical mistake may have an administrative correction route; a genuine dispute over rights may require adjudication before the competent forum. The type of problem determines the remedy.

Do not treat the receipt of an application as proof that the requested entry has been allowed. Track the order and resulting record. Read our Lucknow certified land records guide for building an authenticated record file.

Example: inherited land with several recorded holders

Suppose a seller shows a khatauni containing four family members and offers a marked corner of the holding. The first question is not whether the seller's name appears; it is what interest the seller holds and whether that particular corner has been lawfully allotted or separated.

The record may show a joint holding while the physical marking reflects only an informal family arrangement. Obtain the acquisition and succession material, shares and any partition document or order. Then connect the offered land to the map and the seller's legal interest.

Joint-holding example — questions the record does and does not answer
QuestionWhat the khatauni may showWhat to examine next
Is the seller recorded?A name associated with the holding and relevant entry particulars.Identity, acquisition or succession basis and the actual share held.
Does the seller own the marked corner?The record may identify a joint parcel without an exclusive corner allocation.Partition, allotment or other legally effective material establishing the particular portion.

This example also explains why area calculations need care. A seller's fractional share in a large parcel is not automatically the same as exclusive ownership of an equally sized rectangle at the roadside. Location, access and the legal form of the interest matter.

For an inheritance update, preserve the succession application, notices, order and resulting entry where relevant. Comparing only the latest names can hide the event that changed the record. A chronological file makes it easier to understand whether an omission is administrative, documentary or a substantive rights dispute.

The best use of Bhulekh is therefore investigative: start with the entry, identify the supporting event and obtain the documents that establish its effect. That method remains useful even when the portal interface changes.

Frequently asked questions

Is khatauni the same as a sale deed?

No. A khatauni is a revenue record; the registered deed records the relevant transaction. Read them together and establish the underlying rights instead of treating either one-line entry as a complete title opinion.

Can I find land using only the owner's name?

A supported name search can help locate possible records, but the result needs confirmation through location, parcel, area and acquisition information. A similar name is not proof of a match.

Does an online printout become certified when I sign it?

No. Certification or electronic authentication comes from the competent issuing process. Your signature, a screenshot or a colour print does not change an informational record into an officially certified copy.

What if my village or historical record is not available online?

Identify the competent revenue office and the prescribed route for the required record or period. Document the unavailable online search and request the specific record rather than assuming no record exists.

Official links and a useful land-record file

Use UP Bhulekh for the relevant revenue record service, the UP Board of Revenue for official revenue information and the Lucknow district website for district service gateways. For purchase decisions, combine the record search with our Lucknow title verification guide.

A useful parcel file identifies the land, records the source and date of each entry, explains the title links and lists unresolved discrepancies. That approach turns an online search into practical information for a sale, inheritance, loan or record correction.
