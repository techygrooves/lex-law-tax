Succession certificate in Lucknow: choosing the right legal route

When a person dies, the family is often left with a second set of problems alongside the loss: a bank account cannot be settled, a fixed deposit is in the deceased person's name, shares are held in a demat account, or money owed to the deceased remains unpaid. The surviving spouse, children or other relatives may know who the legal heirs are, yet the institution holding the money may still need satisfactory authority before making payment.

A succession certificate lawyer in Lucknow can help examine whether a court-issued certificate is the appropriate way to collect those particular assets. The answer depends on what the deceased owned, whether there is a nominee or Will, which personal law applies, the institution's settlement process and whether the relatives agree. It is usually sensible to inspect the institution's written requirements before starting court proceedings.

A succession certificate is not the same as a legal-heir certificate, a probate grant or ownership papers for a house. This page explains the differences, the procedure under the Indian Succession Act, 1925 and the records useful for families dealing with assets in Lucknow and elsewhere.

What is a succession certificate under Indian law?

Part X of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 contains the statutory framework for succession certificates. Broadly, it allows a competent court to authorise a person to collect specified debts and securities belonging to a deceased person's estate. The court identifies those debts and securities in the certificate rather than declaring the recipient to be the exclusive owner of everything left behind.

Think of it as authority to approach an institution for payment or dealing with listed securities, subject to the terms of the grant. Under Section 381, a good-faith payment made to the holder in respect of the stated debt or security can protect the person or institution making that payment. That protection is an important practical reason succession certificates exist.

It does not necessarily mean the holder may keep the entire amount personally. Section 387 preserves the ability to determine substantive rights in another competent proceeding and the holder's liability to account to those lawfully entitled. For example, a certificate issued to one of three heirs for an account does not automatically extinguish the inheritance rights of the other two.

An example from an ordinary family

Suppose a Lucknow resident dies leaving a savings account and company shares. The surviving family members disagree about who should collect the amounts, but the bank and company want reliable authority. A succession petition may provide a route for one suitable person to collect the specified assets. The ultimate division among heirs remains a separate question governed by the facts, a valid Will where applicable and the relevant law of succession.

Which assets can a succession certificate cover?

The first job is to identify the actual asset. Section 370 addresses debts and securities; it is not an all-purpose inheritance provision. Common situations in which a certificate may be relevant include:

  • Bank deposits: money payable by a bank on a savings account, current account or fixed deposit, where an institution requires court authority after examining its deceased-customer procedure.
  • Company shares: shares and related securities of a company, and related rights to payment or transfer when covered by the certificate and applicable financial rules.
  • Government or other qualifying securities: securities falling within the statutory categories, including specified government instruments.
  • Money legally owed to the deceased: an identifiable debt recoverable by the estate, subject to the nature of the claim and applicable statutory limits.
  • Investment claims: certain financial products may require an examination of their legal structure, nominee provisions and whether the particular instrument is a debt or security for Part X purposes.

It is important not to assume that every insurance policy, provident-fund benefit, pension, gratuity amount, mutual-fund unit or retirement benefit automatically needs a succession certificate. Special statutory schemes and settlement rules can alter the correct claimant and procedure. Some benefits have their own nomination and beneficiary provisions. Review the particular scheme and any refusal letter before proceeding.

What it does not cover

A certificate under Part X is not a deed transferring a residential house, a plot, agricultural land or a shop. It does not replace a registered conveyance, property-title examination, a partition decree, a revenue succession/mutation process, or an appropriate declaratory proceeding. Jewellery or physical personal belongings likewise raise different ownership and estate-administration questions.

Succession certificate, legal-heir certificate, probate and mutation: the differences

Families often receive inconsistent advice because all these documents are loosely described as “inheritance certificates”. They are not interchangeable.

Document or proceedingWhat it is mainly used forWhat it does not establish
Succession certificateAuthority to collect specified debts and deal with specified securities under Part X.Final ownership of land, houses or even the entire estate.
Legal-heir or surviving-family certificateAdministrative identification of family members for particular government or institutional processes, where issued.Automatic adjudication of disputed title or a substitute for every court-required document.
ProbateJudicial grant concerning a Will and the appointed executor, in cases where probate is maintainable and appropriate.Final resolution of every independent property-title dispute.
Letters of administrationAuthority to administer an estate in circumstances provided by law.The same thing as a narrow debt-and-securities certificate.
Mutation / dakhil kharijUpdates relevant land, revenue or municipal records.Conclusive ownership merely by entry of a person's name.

If the real dispute is over ownership of a house, the answer will not ordinarily be to file a succession certificate petition for that house. If the issue is a bank refusing settlement of a deceased customer's deposit, a title suit over immovable property may be equally misplaced. Identifying the asset and the obstacle first saves considerable unnecessary expense.

Who can apply for a succession certificate?

People commonly applying include a surviving husband or wife, son, daughter, parent or another person able to show a legally relevant interest. Eligibility is not determined by a simple rule that only the eldest son can file. A married daughter is not disqualified merely because she is married. Equally, a sibling or distant relative does not automatically become entitled when closer heirs may exist.

Under Section 372, the petition must state the right in which the applicant claims and identify family or other near relatives and their residences. The court examines the competing claims under Section 373. Where several people seek the certificate, the court can consider their respective interests and suitability; the proceeding is summary rather than a full trial of every inheritance question.

For a Hindu family, the Hindu Succession Act and relevant facts concerning the estate may be important. For a Muslim family, applicable Muslim succession principles must be examined; Hindu coparcenary rules should not simply be imported. Other personal laws, valid testamentary dispositions and applicable special statutes may lead to different answers. No reliable share calculation is possible without the complete family tree and relevant dates.

Can several heirs apply together?

More than one person may be interested in obtaining authority. Whether a single applicant, joint applicants or another arrangement is appropriate depends on the petition, court procedure and circumstances. A no-objection statement from another heir can be helpful, but it is not a substitute for accurately disclosing all relevant heirs, and the court is not bound to grant a certificate merely because relatives sign a consent document.

Which court can issue a succession certificate in Lucknow?

Section 371 ordinarily connects jurisdiction to the place where the deceased ordinarily resided at the time of death. If the deceased then had no fixed place of residence, the provision refers to the jurisdiction in which part of the deceased's property may be found. Section 388 also permits qualifying courts to be invested with powers under Part X through the applicable legal mechanism.

Accordingly, a deceased person having a Lucknow bank account does not, by itself, prove that a Lucknow court is the correct forum if the person ordinarily lived elsewhere. Equally, the applicant's current address is not necessarily decisive. The deceased's residence history, relevant account details and the court's jurisdiction should be checked before filing.

Applications connected with Lucknow ordinarily concern the competent civil or district court exercising succession jurisdiction, not a routine petition directly before the Allahabad High Court's Lucknow Bench. The particular filing court, assignment and local practice should be confirmed with current court rules. The High Court may become relevant in the appellate or supervisory context, depending on the order and governing law.

Succession certificate procedure in Lucknow: step by step

1. Identify the debts, securities and institution's requirements

Collect the bank's or company's written claim requirements, details of each account, statements if available and the institution's response to the deceased-account settlement request. Check whether a nominee exists and whether the institution can process the claim under its rules without a succession certificate. Record account and security particulars carefully; a vague reference to “all assets” is generally unhelpful.

2. Verify the death, family tree and right to apply

Prepare the death certificate, details of the deceased's usual residence, names and addresses of close relatives, a family tree, and documents connecting the applicant to the deceased. A previous Will, probate proceeding, family settlement or rival claim must be considered rather than concealed.

3. Draft the petition under Section 372

The petition is signed and verified as required by the statute and sets out the death, residence, near relatives, claimed right, relevant absence of impediments and the debts and securities sought. Supporting affidavits, schedules of assets and other documents are arranged according to the competent court's rules. Statements must be accurate: incorrect declarations can have serious consequences.

4. Court scrutiny and notice

If the judge finds grounds to entertain the application, Section 373 provides for notice to persons who should receive special notice, posting at the courthouse and such other publication as the court directs under applicable rules. Some proceedings involve newspaper publication; it is inaccurate to describe one fixed publication format as compulsory in every case.

5. Hearing, objections and evidence

The court considers the applicant's prima facie right and the evidence. Relatives may support the application or object, for example on the basis of an omitted heir, a disputed Will or competing entitlement. Where difficult questions of fact or law would require a more extensive suit, Section 373 still permits a grant to the person appearing to have prima facie the best title in circumstances contemplated by the statute.

6. Court fee, security and issuance

Sections 375 and 379 address security or bonds and the collection of court fees. The applicable Uttar Pradesh fee schedule, valuation and court directions must be checked. The certificate specifies the approved debts and securities and may contain powers regarding dividends, interest or transfer of securities, under Section 374.

7. Submit the certificate and account for collections

After receiving the appropriate certified document, the holder approaches the relevant bank or institution and completes its prescribed formalities. Further identity, tax, indemnity, KYC or transmission requirements may remain. Receipt of funds does not automatically determine how those funds should be divided among the persons lawfully entitled.

Documents required for a succession certificate

Exact requirements depend on the estate and court. A practical checklist for a succession certificate application in Lucknow is:

  • Death evidence: official death certificate, and any explanation or order needed if the record is delayed or inconsistent.
  • Deceased's residence: address evidence and particulars of where the deceased ordinarily lived immediately before death.
  • Applicant identification: appropriate proof of name, residence and identity, and documents showing the relationship or claimed right.
  • All close relatives: family tree, correct names, addresses, and relevant records of earlier deaths or changed family circumstances.
  • Asset schedule: bank name and branch, account or FD details, estimated balances, company or depository details, share particulars, securities statements or correspondence.
  • Nomination records: nominee details, where available, and documents showing any claim already submitted or amount paid.
  • Will or other instruments: original or copies of any available Will, earlier court grant, family arrangement or proceeding relevant to the application.
  • Institution communication: written request, refusal, deficiency letter or other response from the bank, company or fund administrator.
  • Court documents: duly prepared petition, required affidavit or verification, schedule of debts and securities, and consent or no-objection documents if relevant and genuinely given.

If the account numbers or share details are incomplete, begin by lawfully requesting statements or claim information from the institution. Do not estimate a bank balance and present it as confirmed; the petition should make clear which figures are supported and which still need verification.

Does a nominee need a succession certificate?

Not necessarily. Many financial institutions have procedures for payment to a nominee or settlement without a court order in qualifying circumstances. Their requirements depend on the relevant law, scheme rules, customer records and whether a dispute or competing claim has been raised. Asking for the institution's current written deceased-claim policy is often the most productive starting point.

Nomination and ultimate beneficial ownership are separate concepts in many contexts. In Shakti Yezdani v. Jayanand Jayant Salgaonkar (2023), the Supreme Court examined nomination of company securities and held that the particular nomination regime did not displace the law of succession in the way claimed by the nominees. This does not mean every statutory nomination scheme operates identically; the applicable provision must be examined.

If a bank has already made a payment to a nominee, the next question may concern the nominee's obligations towards other heirs. A succession certificate is not a universal mechanism for reversing completed payments or conclusively determining the nominee's beneficial rights. The nature of the dispute may require a different proceeding.

What if one heir objects or refuses to cooperate?

One relative's refusal to sign a no-objection certificate does not automatically end the possibility of a court application. The person seeking the certificate must nevertheless disclose the relevant heirs, provide evidence of the claimed right and address the objection through the court's procedure. An objection may raise genuine issues of succession, the existence of a Will, the identity of the deceased, or the accuracy of the asset schedule.

Because the Part X proceeding is summary, the judge does not necessarily conduct a complete partition or inheritance trial. A grant in favour of one applicant is not a blanket judgment against the substantive rights of other heirs. Section 387 permits the underlying questions of right to be addressed in separate competent proceedings and preserves obligations to account.

Where there are allegations of forged documents, concealed heirs or wrongful transfers, preserve the available evidence promptly. A contested case should not be presented as certain to finish within a fixed number of weeks or months.

How much are succession certificate court fees in Uttar Pradesh?

The amount is not a flat legal-heir-certificate charge. Court fee for the certificate is linked to the amount or value of the covered debts and securities under the applicable court-fees legislation and State amendments. The relevant valuation, classification and current Uttar Pradesh schedule should be verified when preparing the petition. Additional expenses can include process service, publication where ordered, certified copies, security compliance and professional fees.

Section 379 of the Indian Succession Act specifically addresses the collection of court fees in certificate proceedings. Because older all-India online calculators sometimes display central rates rather than the applicable State position, do not rely on a quoted percentage from a generic website without checking the governing schedule and the court's current practice.

A fee assessment should begin with an accurate asset schedule: what amounts are actually payable, which assets fall within Part X and what value is claimed. If a high-value certificate is proposed, the cost of the court route should be compared with any lawful institution-level claim process before filing.

How long does the succession certificate process take?

There is no honest universal completion promise. The time needed depends on the correctness of the initial petition, court workload, notice and publication directions, availability of supporting statements, objections from relatives, evidence and compliance with the final order. An uncontested application may be simpler than one involving competing Wills or concealed heirs, but even an uncontested matter has formal steps.

The court's order and the institution's later settlement are also different stages. Receiving a certificate is not necessarily equivalent to receiving the money that same day. A bank or depository may need to verify the grant and complete lawful procedures before payment or transmission. Families should plan for those separate stages.

Succession certificates for Hindus, Muslims and other families

Part X provides a certificate procedure; the underlying question of who is lawfully entitled to an estate may involve different personal laws. In a Hindu family, the family tree, class of heirs, any valid Will and the property held by the deceased require examination under the relevant legal framework. Merely being the eldest child does not automatically give exclusive rights to collect or retain all financial assets.

For a Muslim deceased person, heirs' interests must be analysed under applicable Muslim personal-law principles, including questions concerning surviving spouse, children, parents and other relatives. A Muslim succession problem should not be addressed through an assumption that Hindu coparcenary rules apply. The presence of a nominee should be considered separately from the heirs' substantive inheritance rights.

In Christian or other succession cases, the statutory and personal-law framework may operate differently. The careful approach is to identify the deceased's applicable law, any testamentary document, the exact assets and statutory restrictions before asserting a right to a particular share or a certificate.

What if the deceased left a Will?

A Will changes the questions that need to be addressed but does not mean that an application under Part X should always be rejected or always accepted. The terms, genuineness and legal operation of the Will, whether an executor is appointed, any existing probate or letters of administration and the specific debts and securities all matter.

The Repealing and Amending Act, 2025 removed Section 213 of the Indian Succession Act and amended Section 370 to remove its former reference to Section 213, among other changes. As a result, older descriptions of a blanket requirement for probate in order to pursue succession certificate relief should not be repeated without accounting for the amendment. Section 370 still contains a restriction concerning debts and securities for which rights are required by Section 212 to be established through letters of administration.

For a family in Lucknow, the sensible first step is to review the Will and determine which court or administrative process actually addresses the financial asset. If the Will itself is seriously challenged, the dispute may demand separate testamentary or civil litigation. A succession certificate does not, simply by being issued, conclusively establish the genuineness of a Will for every purpose.

What if new bank accounts or securities are discovered later?

People often find another fixed deposit, dividend statement or shareholding months after the initial paperwork. Section 376 of the Indian Succession Act provides for an application to extend an existing succession certificate to additional debts or securities. The extension requires the appropriate court process and may involve additional fees or security; it should not be assumed that the original grant silently includes every later-discovered asset.

For that reason, prepare an asset inventory early and keep copies of the responses received from banks, companies and financial intermediaries. A careful schedule can reduce duplicate proceedings and make later compliance easier.

Can a succession certificate be challenged, revoked or appealed?

Yes, the statute provides safeguards. Section 383 lists circumstances in which a certificate may be revoked, including substantial defects in the proceedings, material fraudulent concealment, certain untrue essential allegations and other specified grounds. Section 384 provides for an appeal from orders granting, refusing or revoking a certificate, subject to the applicable jurisdictional framework and limitations.

For example, deliberately omitting a daughter or widow from the family particulars may be material; however, whether the omission justifies a particular remedy depends on the evidence and legal test. A disappointed relative does not establish fraud merely by disagreeing with the court's conclusion. Delay and the procedural stage matter, so a person who discovers a problem should obtain the petition and order promptly.

Where the holder has already collected funds, a challenge to the grant and a claim for accounting or distribution may involve different legal issues. Preserve bank records and communications rather than assuming revocation alone necessarily recovers money.

Succession certificate or property inheritance in Lucknow?

The distinction is especially important where a family has both financial accounts and land or buildings. A deceased person might leave an apartment in Gomti Nagar, an ancestral share in a rural holding and deposits at a city bank. Those assets cannot simply be grouped into one “succession certificate for everything”.

The bank deposits may raise a Part X question. The apartment may call for title verification, an inheritance or Will analysis, relevant mutation and, if contested, appropriate civil proceedings. Agricultural holdings in Uttar Pradesh can be governed by different revenue and succession provisions, depending on the land's status and facts. A certificate for bank debts does not convert itself into an agricultural-land mutation order.

Frequently asked questions about succession certificates in Lucknow

1. Is a succession certificate mandatory to withdraw money from a deceased parent's bank account?

Not in every case. The bank may have a settlement procedure based on nomination, survivorship provisions, claim amounts and required documents. A court certificate may become relevant when the institution legitimately requires it or a competing claim creates difficulty. Ask the bank for its applicable deceased-account procedure and a written list of deficiencies before deciding on litigation.

2. Can a married daughter apply for a succession certificate?

Marriage does not by itself disqualify a daughter from applying. Her legal interest and suitability depend on the family circumstances, personal law, any Will and the purpose of the application. Other surviving heirs must be disclosed where relevant. The certificate's recipient is not automatically the sole beneficial owner of the proceeds.

3. Can I obtain a succession certificate for my father's house or agricultural land?

A Part X succession certificate concerns specified debts and securities. It does not serve as the ownership document for a house, plot or agricultural holding. For immovable property, examine the title chain, applicable succession law, mutation procedure and any dispute over partition or possession.

4. Can one brother get the certificate without telling his sisters?

The petition under Section 372 requires particulars of family or near relatives, and Section 373 addresses notice. Material concealment can create serious legal issues, including potential revocation under Section 383. A family member who learns of an undisclosed petition should seek the papers and consider an appropriate response.

5. Does an existing nominee automatically own all bank deposits or shares?

No universal rule makes every nominee the beneficial owner regardless of inheritance law. Nomination often facilitates a financial institution's settlement, while beneficial entitlement may remain governed by other law. The exact statute and scheme matter. The Supreme Court's decision in Shakti Yezdani is especially relevant to the nomination regime considered there.

6. Is a no-objection certificate from all heirs compulsory?

Consent can simplify proof and reduce contest, but the statute provides for court examination and notice. The exact procedure depends on the court and circumstances. A relative's refusal is not an automatic legal veto, and fabricated or pressured no-objection documents should never be used.

7. Can an applicant claim shares and fixed deposits in one petition?

Potentially, if the assets fall within the statutory category and are sufficiently identified, and the court otherwise has jurisdiction. Prepare an accurate schedule of each debt or security, with the relevant company or institution details and supporting records. The court specifies the covered assets in the certificate.

8. How is a succession certificate different from a legal-heir certificate?

A succession certificate is a judicial grant under Part X for identified debts and securities. Administrative legal-heir or surviving-family documents are used for specific departmental purposes and have a different basis. A legal-heir certificate is not automatically a court order authorising collection of every disputed bank account or shareholding.

9. What are the succession certificate fees in Lucknow?

Fees depend on the applicable Uttar Pradesh court-fee law and the value and type of assets covered. Additional expenses and professional charges depend on the proceeding. It would be misleading to quote one flat statutory amount for all families without checking the current fee schedule and asset schedule.

10. How many months does it take to receive the certificate?

No guaranteed time applies. Court scrutiny, notices, publication if ordered, contested claims, evidence and fee/security compliance can affect the case. After issuance, an institution may also need time to process the claim. Ask for a realistic case-specific assessment after the papers are reviewed.

11. What if the deceased had a Will?

The Will, any executor, its legal status and existing grants must be examined. The 2025 amendment omitted Section 213 and changed Section 370, but the remaining restrictions and other applicable provisions still matter. A certificate proceeding is not a substitute for every issue involving the validity or interpretation of a Will.

12. Can the person named in a succession certificate keep the whole amount?

Not merely because their name appears on the certificate. The grant chiefly facilitates collecting the specified debts and securities. The substantive entitlement of other heirs and the grantee's obligation to account can remain open, including under Section 387. Distribution must follow applicable law and any effective testamentary or judicial directions.

13. What if another deposit is found after the succession certificate is issued?

Section 376 provides a mechanism to apply for extension of the certificate to additional debts or securities. Fresh disclosure, valuation and fee or security requirements may apply. An existing certificate should not be assumed to cover an account that is not listed in it.

14. Can an incorrectly obtained succession certificate be cancelled?

Section 383 provides specified grounds for revocation, including material fraud and certain substantial defects. There may also be appellate remedies under Section 384. The appropriate route depends on the order, evidence, date of knowledge and the actual ground of challenge.

Primary legal sources and further reading

The statutory provisions and judgments below are useful starting points. Legal requirements and local court practice should be rechecked against current authoritative material when preparing a particular case.

  • Indian Succession Act, 1925 — especially Part X, Sections 370–390; the PDF is a base text and should be read with later amendments.
  • Repealing and Amending Act, 2025 — changes to Sections 213 and 370 of the Indian Succession Act.
  • Shakti Yezdani v. Jayanand Jayant Salgaonkar, Supreme Court (2023) — nomination and succession to company securities under the legal regime considered.
  • Judicial discussion of Supreme Court authorities including Banarsi Dass and Madhvi Amma — summary character and limited legal effect of succession certificates.
